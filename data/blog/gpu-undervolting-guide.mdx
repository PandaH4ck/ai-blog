Modern gaming laptops often push mobile GPUs to their thermal limits...

Undervolting allows you to lock consistent frequencies at significantly lower core voltages without sacrificing raw computing power.

---

### Why Undervolt?

- **Thermal Headroom:** Dropping voltage by 50–100 mV can lower core temperatures by 5–12°C.
- **Sustained Boost Clocks:** Eliminates thermal throttling, maintaining a flat clock speed under heavy loads.
- **Acoustics & Longevity:** Cooler temperatures reduce cooling fan wear and high-RPM noise.

---

### Required Software

1. **MSI Afterburner:** For editing the voltage-frequency curve.
2. **HWiNFO64:** For monitoring core temperatures, power consumption (W), and thermal throttle flags.
3. **Stress Testing Tools:** 3DMark Time Spy, Superposition Benchmark, or any GPU-heavy title run in a loop.

---

### Step-by-Step Configuration

#### 1. Establish the Baseline

Run a benchmark run (e.g., 3DMark Time Spy) at stock settings. Record:

- Peak GPU temperature.
- Average sustained clock speed.
- Total board power (TGP) draw.

#### 2. Open the Voltage/Frequency Curve

In MSI Afterburner, hit **`Ctrl + F`** to open the curve editor.

Identify your target boost clock. For a typical laptop GPU (like an RTX 3060 Mobile), a solid starting target is around **1600–1700 MHz** at roughly **750–775 mV**.

#### 3. Flatten the High Voltage Range

1. Select the point on the curve at your target voltage (e.g., `750 mV`).
2. Raise that point to your desired clock frequency (e.g., `1600 MHz`).
3. Flatten all points to the right of your target so the card never requests higher voltages.
4. Click the checkmark in MSI Afterburner to apply the profile.

---

### Stability Testing

Run 20–30 minutes of continuous stress testing.

- **If it crashes or shows artifacts:** Raise the voltage by 15–25 mV (e.g., move from 750 mV to 775 mV) or lower the core clock by 25–50 MHz.
- **If it passes without errors:** Lock the profile to start with Windows.
